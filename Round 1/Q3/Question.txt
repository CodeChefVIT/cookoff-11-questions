Chef is renovating his restaurant's courtyard after the storm. The courtyard floor is uneven, represented as N adjacent unit-width tiles, where the i-th tile has a raised lip of height H_i running along its edge. After the storm, rain settles into every gap between lips, constrained only by the tallest lip visible looking left and the tallest lip visible looking right from that tile — water flows off the ends of the courtyard freely and does not pool beyond the first and last tile.

Chef does not want to know the deepest single puddle, nor the best two lips to dam between — he wants the total volume of water resting across the entire courtyard, summed over every tile, after the storm settles and no more water is added or drained.

Note that a tile with a lip taller than both the maximum height seen so far on its left and its right holds no water at all, and consecutive tiles of equal height must still each be checked individually — do not assume a single global maximum is sufficient to compute the answer for tiles on both sides of it in one pass without tracking direction separately.
